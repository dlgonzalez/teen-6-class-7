Grammar: Will vs Going To

Two ways to talk about the future — when do we use each?

What you're about to do: Review the two most common future forms in English with summary cards, then complete practice exercises and a pair activity.
Why: Knowing WHEN to use will vs going to is essential for natural English.

W

WILL

📌 Use it for...

  • ✅ Instant decisions — made at the moment of speaking.
  • ✅ Predictions — based on opinion or belief (no evidence).
  • ✅ Promises & offers — helping, agreeing, refusing.
  • ✅ Facts about the future — things we can't control.

🔧 Structure

+ Subject + will + verb
I will help you.

− Subject + won't + verb
She won't come.

? Will + subject + verb?
Will you help me?

💬 Sample sentences

🔹 "The phone is ringing. — I'll get it!" (instant decision)

🔹 "I think it will rain tomorrow." (prediction)

🔹 "I will always love you." (promise)

🔹 "Don't worry — everything will be fine." (reassurance)

G

GOING TO

📌 Use it for...

  • ✅ Plans & intentions — decided before speaking.
  • ✅ Predictions based on evidence — we can SEE it coming.
  • ✅ Arrangements — things already organized.

🔧 Structure

+ Subject + am/is/are + going to + verb
I'm going to study tonight.

− Subject + am/is/are + not going to + verb
They aren't going to travel.

? Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + verb?
Are you going to come?

💬 Sample sentences

🔹 "I'm going to visit Japan next summer." (plan)

🔹 "Look at those clouds! It's going to rain." (evidence)

🔹 "We're going to have a party on Saturday." (arrangement)

🔹 "She's not going to accept that offer." (intention)

Model: How to Answer the Questions

Study this example before writing your own

Below is a sample student's answers about their future. Notice how they mix will and going to depending on the situation.

Q1What are your plans for next summer?

Sample answer: "Next summer, I'm going to visit my grandparents in the countryside. We're going to stay there for two weeks. I think it'll be really fun because we always have a good time together."

💡 Why this works: Uses going to for plans already decided, and will for a prediction (opinion).

Q2What do you think life will be like in 20 years?

Sample answer: "In 20 years, I think cars will drive themselves and people will live longer. I hope I'll have a good job and a happy family. Technology will probably change a lot!"

💡 Why this works: Uses will for predictions about the future — opinions and beliefs, not plans.

Q3What are you going to do this weekend?

Sample answer: "This weekend, I'm going to study for my English test on Friday. On Saturday, my friends and I are going to watch a movie. I'll probably sleep a lot too!"

💡 Why this works: Uses going to for already-decided plans, and will + probably for something not fully decided.

Q4What career do you want to have?

Sample answer: "I'm going to be a graphic designer. I've already started learning design on YouTube. I'll take some art classes next year to get better. I hope I'll work for a big company one day."

💡 Why this works: Uses going to for a clear intention, and will for a future hope/prediction.

Q5Where do you want to travel in the future?

Sample answer: "I really want to travel to Japan. I'm going to save money for two years, and then I'm going to buy a plane ticket. I'll probably visit Tokyo and Kyoto. I think the food will be amazing!"

💡 Why this works: Uses going to for planned steps, and will for personal predictions.

Tips for Good Writing

1. Mix the tenses! Don't use only will or only going to. Show you understand the difference.

2. Add reasons or details. Don't just say "I'll travel." Say "I'll travel to Japan because I love Japanese culture."

3. Use connecting words. Link your ideas with: because, and, but, also, so, then, after that.

4. Watch for common mistakes. ❌ "I will to go" → ✅ "I will go". ❌ "I'm go to travel" → ✅ "I'm going to travel".

5. Use adverbs for accuracy. Add probably, definitely, maybe to show how sure you are: "I'll probably visit..." / "I'll definitely try..."

Help Phrases You Can Use

For plans & intentions (going to)

I'm going to... We're planning to... I've decided to... My plan is to... I'm thinking of...

For predictions & hopes (will)

I think I'll... I hope I'll... I'll probably... Maybe I'll... I'm sure I'll...

For adding reasons & details

...because... ...so I can... ...which means... For example,... Also,...

Listening Test

An individual test — no talking, just listening!

What you're about to do: Complete a listening test on your own. Listen to the audio, answer the questions, and submit your worksheet with your name.
Why: This is an assessment — it shows how much you understand and helps your teacher see what to work on next.

Tips for Good Listening

1. Read the questions first. Before the audio starts, read every question. This tells your brain what to listen for.

2. Predict the type of answer. Is it a number? A name? A place? A time? Knowing this helps you focus.

3. Listen for keywords, not every word. You don't need to understand everything — just the important information.

4. Don't panic if you miss something. Skip it and move to the next question. You can come back if the audio repeats.

5. Watch out for distractors. Sometimes the speaker mentions a wrong answer first, then corrects it. Listen for words like actually, but, no wait.

6. Check your spelling. Names, numbers, and days of the week must be written correctly.

Important: Write Your Full Name!

Before you start, make sure you write your full name on the worksheet. The teacher needs to know whose test this is!

Speaking: Plan Your Dream Trip

Work with a partner to plan an amazing trip — using future tenses and travel vocabulary!

What you're about to do: In pairs, you'll plan a 1-week dream trip — choosing a destination, transportation, dates, and activities. Then you'll present it to the class.
Why: This brings together EVERYTHING from today: travel vocabulary, phrasal verbs, future tenses, and present tenses with future meaning. It's your chance to actually use the language, not just study it.

Step 1: Prepare Your Trip (5 min)

Decide these things with your partner. Write short notes — you'll present them in a moment!

🌍 Destination

Where are you going? Why?

✈️ Transportation

How will you get there? Use: by + transport, take off, set off

📅 Dates & Times

When do you leave? Use simple present for schedules!

🎒 Activities

What will you do there? Use going to, will, might

💭 Bonus: One Surprise

Add one thing that is possible but not certain (use might).

Useful Phrases for Your Presentation

✈️ Plans (going to):

  • • We're going to visit...
  • • We're going to stay for...
  • • We're going to travel by...

📅 Schedules (present):

  • • Our plane leaves at...
  • • The train arrives at...
  • • We're meeting at...

🤔 Possibilities (might):

  • • We might also...
  • • We might not have time to...
  • • If we're lucky, we'll...
